## Page 1 — Early life, education, and career foundation

Johnathan Hale, known to colleagues simply as **John**, is a systems reliability engineer and technical writer based in Chicago. Friends describe him as methodical, unusually patient with ambiguous requirements, and quietly competitive about incident postmortems. John grew up in a bilingual household near Milwaukee, splitting weekends between lake sailing and library stacks. Teachers marked him early as “the kid who annotated everything.” In high school he captained the robotics club, rebuilt a donated CNC mill for the shop class, and kept a paper notebook of failure modes for every machine the lab owned. Summer jobs included warehouse inventory audits and a municipal GIS internship that taught him how messy real-world data becomes once people are involved.

He enrolled at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, majoring in Computer Engineering with a minor in Technical Communication. Coursework spanned digital design, operating systems, human factors, and rhetoric for engineers. Capstone work focused on fault injection for embedded controllers used in campus building automation. Outside class he volunteered as a peer tutor for first-year programming and contributed release notes for an open-source packet-capture utility.

After graduation John joined a mid-size logistics software firm as a junior platform engineer. His first year covered on-call rotations, runbook authorship, and migrating a brittle Jenkins estate to containerized workers. Mentors pushed him to write “why” sections, not only “how” checklists. By year three he was leading weekly reliability reviews, measuring SLO burn rates, and translating executive risk language into backlog tickets developers could actually ship.

John’s working style favors short written briefs before meetings, diagrams that survive photocopying, and a bias for reversible changes. He keeps a personal glossary of domain terms because he has watched too many incidents grow from two teams using the same word differently. Colleagues say he will happily spend an hour aligning vocabulary if it saves a day of thrash later.

Community involvement includes mentoring university interns twice a year, judging a regional high-school hackathon, and publishing occasional blog posts on on-call ergonomics. He reads widely: incident reports from aviation and medicine, essays on documentation debt, and—somewhat unexpectedly—mid-century industrial photography books. Music taste leans toward long-form instrumental albums during deep work blocks.

John currently lives in a walk-up apartment with two overwatered plants, a standing desk that squeaks, and a whiteboard covered in unfinished dependency graphs. He cycles to the office when weather allows, takes notes on paper first, and retypes them into the company wiki the same evening so search stays honest.
